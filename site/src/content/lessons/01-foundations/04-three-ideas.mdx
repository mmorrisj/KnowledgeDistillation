These three terms are often used loosely, sometimes as if they meant the same thing. They do not. Each answers a different question, and keeping them apart will save you from confused designs and confused conversations.

## One sentence each

- **Fine-tuning** is training a pretrained model further on examples of your task.
- **Distillation** is training a student model on targets that come from a teacher model.
- **LoRA** is a way of fine-tuning that freezes the original weights and trains a small set of added matrices.

<div class="table-wrap">

| | Fine-tuning | Distillation | LoRA |
|---|---|---|---|
| Question it answers | How do I adapt a pretrained model? | Where do the targets come from? | Which weights are allowed to change? |
| What it changes in the loop | The data and the starting weights | The target | The update |
| What you end up with | A full copy of the model with new weights | A student trained on a teacher's outputs | A small adapter file that sits on top of the base model |
| Needs a teacher model? | No | Yes | No |

</div>

## How they fit together

Distillation is fine-tuning with a different source of targets. When you distill, you are still fine-tuning the student; the labels simply come from a teacher.

LoRA is fine-tuning with a different update. When people say "full fine-tuning" they mean every weight moves, as opposed to LoRA.

So a single training run can be all three at once: you fine-tune a student (the activity), on a teacher's outputs (distillation), by training only adapters (LoRA).

## Common mix-ups

> "We distilled the model with LoRA."

This may be true, but it describes two separate choices. Ask what the teacher was. If there was no teacher, it was LoRA fine-tuning on human labels, and nothing was distilled.

> "LoRA makes the model smaller."

It does not. LoRA makes training cheaper. The model you run afterwards is the same size as the base model, plus a small adapter. Distilling into a smaller student is what makes the model smaller.

> "Distillation needs the teacher's weights."

Only white-box distillation does. Training on a teacher's text answers through an API is also distillation.
