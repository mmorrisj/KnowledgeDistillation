A team distills a model and reports: "Our student scores 82%." Is that good?

You cannot tell. If the same small model scores 81% when trained on the team's own labels, the teacher added almost nothing. If it scores 60%, the teacher added a great deal. One number on its own says very little.

## The three numbers

Every distillation result in this course is reported as three scores, measured on the same test set.

- **The teacher.** What the large model scores on the task. This is what you are aiming at.
- **The baseline.** What the student scores when trained without the teacher, on whatever human labels you have. This is where you start.
- **The distilled student.** What the student scores after learning from the teacher.

This module produces the first two. Modules 3 and 4 produce the third.

## Gap closed

The useful summary is how much of the distance from the baseline to the teacher the student covered.

> gap closed = (distilled − baseline) ÷ (teacher − baseline)

A student that closes 100% of the gap matches the teacher. One that closes 0% gained nothing from it.

## When the teacher is not better

<PredictReveal
  client:load
  id="who-wins"
  prompt="You have 10,000 messages labelled by people. Model A is a small encoder trained on all of them. Model B is a 7 billion parameter model that answers from a prompt, with no training. On 77 fine-grained types, which usually scores higher?"
  options={["The large prompted model", "The small trained model", "They score about the same"]}
>
  Usually the small trained model. Thousands of good labels teach a narrow task very well, and a prompt alone often cannot match that on fine-grained types. You will measure this yourself in the lab, so treat this as a prediction to check, not a fact to memorize.
</PredictReveal>

This is the most useful thing to know before starting a distillation project: **if you already have plenty of good labels, you may not need a teacher at all.** Distillation pays off when labels are scarce or expensive and unlabelled examples are plentiful. That describes most real projects, where nobody has labelled ten thousand examples.

## The scenario for this course

So the course sets up the realistic case. The data is Banking77: customer messages to a bank, each belonging to one of 77 types. We pretend that people have labelled only a few of them.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Pile | Size | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Gold training set | 616 (8 per type) | The few messages a person has labelled |
| Gold validation set | 308 (4 per type) | Labelled messages used to make decisions during training |
| Unlabelled pool | about 9,000 | Messages with no human label. The teacher will label these in later modules |
| Test set | 1,000 | Held back. Used only to score finished models |

</div>

The baseline is a student trained on the 616 gold messages. The lab also trains one model on every available label, as a **reference**: it shows what you could reach by paying people to label everything, which is the alternative distillation competes with.
