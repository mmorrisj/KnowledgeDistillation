## What temperature does

The teacher's probabilities come from scores, one per type, passed through softmax. Temperature, written T, divides the scores first:

> probabilities = softmax(scores ÷ T)

- **T = 1** leaves the teacher's distribution as it is.
- **T above 1** flattens it. Second and third choices get more weight, so the student sees more of how the teacher ranks the alternatives.
- **T below 1** sharpens it toward a single answer. As T approaches zero you are back to a plain label.

## Why flatter can be better

At T = 1 a teacher might give 97% to its top choice and split 3% among the rest. The student's loss is then dominated by getting the top choice right, which is the same thing a label teaches. Raise T and the relationships among the other types become large enough to shape what the student learns.

Flatter is not always better. At a very high temperature every type looks about equally likely and the signal is washed out. There is a useful middle, commonly somewhere from 2 to 5, and it depends on the teacher and the task.

One rule keeps the comparison fair: **the student's scores are divided by the same T as the teacher's** while training. At prediction time the student uses T = 1.

## Choosing T

Temperature is a setting, so it is chosen the same way as any other: train with a few values, compare them on the **validation set**, and score only the winner on the test set. You will do exactly this in the lab.
