You have the teacher's probabilities and a temperature. The last piece is the loss: the number the training loop shrinks. In distillation it has two parts.

## Two targets, one loss

- **The teacher term** measures how far the student's softened probabilities are from the teacher's. It uses KL divergence, which is zero when two distributions match and grows as they differ.
- **The label term** is ordinary cross-entropy against the human label, exactly as in fine-tuning. It applies only to messages a person has labelled.

A weight called <mark>alpha</mark> mixes them:

> loss = alpha × teacher term + (1 − alpha) × label term

Move alpha to 0 and you have the Module 2 baseline. Move it to 1 and the human labels are ignored. In between, the student listens to both.

## Why keep the human labels at all

The teacher is not always right. In Module 2 you measured how often it is wrong. Human labels, where you have them, correct the teacher on exactly the messages it gets wrong. In the course scenario the 616 gold messages have both a human label and teacher probabilities, and the roughly 9,000 pool messages have teacher probabilities only. The loss handles both in one batch: pool messages simply contribute nothing to the label term.

## The T² factor

Softening has a side effect. Dividing scores by T makes the gradients of the teacher term smaller, by about a factor of T². Without a correction, raising the temperature would quietly turn down the teacher's influence, and alpha would mean something different at every T.

Multiplying the teacher term by T² cancels this. It keeps the two terms on a comparable scale, so you can tune T and alpha separately.

## The loss in code

```python
def kd_loss(student_logits, teacher_log_probs, hard_labels,
            temperature=2.0, alpha=0.5):
    soft = torch.softmax(teacher_log_probs / temperature, dim=-1)   # 1
    student = F.log_softmax(student_logits / temperature, dim=-1)   # 2
    teacher_term = F.kl_div(student, soft,
                            reduction="batchmean") * temperature**2 # 3

    if (hard_labels != -100).any():                                 # 4
        label_term = F.cross_entropy(student_logits, hard_labels,
                                     ignore_index=-100)
    else:
        label_term = student_logits.sum() * 0.0
    return alpha * teacher_term + (1 - alpha) * label_term          # 5
```

</WorkedExample>

This is the function the lab uses, from `kd_course.distill`. Everything else in the training loop is the same as the fine-tuning loop from Module 2. Distillation changed one line: the target.

## Reasonable starting values

Start with a temperature of 2 and an alpha of 0.5. Then try a higher temperature and a higher alpha, and compare on the validation set. With few human labels and a decent teacher, a higher alpha often does better, because the teacher has scored far more messages than people have labelled.
