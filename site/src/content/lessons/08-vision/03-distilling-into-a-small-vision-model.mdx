The teacher has about 430 million weights and reads a class name for every decision. The student in this module has under 6 million and reads only the image. This lesson covers how the knowledge gets from one to the other.

## The recipe has not changed

1. The teacher scores every photo in the pool and the gold training set. The scores are saved.
2. The student trains on the pool using the teacher's probabilities, and on the gold photos using both the teacher's probabilities and the human labels.
3. The loss is `kd_loss` from lesson 3.4: alpha times the teacher term, plus one minus alpha times the label term, with a temperature and the T² correction.

## Preparing images once

Opening an image file, resizing it and converting it to numbers takes far longer than the equivalent step for a sentence. If that happens again for every image in every epoch, the GPU spends most of its time waiting. The course code prepares each image once and keeps the result in memory. For a few thousand images that is a few gigabytes, which the lab machine has to spare.

## Augmentation

A student with 148 labelled photos will memorize them unless it is shown variety. <mark>Augmentation</mark> makes variety from the images you have: mirror a photo, crop it slightly differently, shift its colors. The label is unchanged, and the student sees something a little new each time.

The course code uses the mildest kind, mirroring half the images in each batch left to right.

>
  For mirroring, no. The teacher's opinion of a photo and of its mirror image are almost identical, so the saved scores still fit. For strong augmentation the answer changes: a crop that cuts off the animal's head, or two photos blended together, is a different picture, and the teacher's scores for the original no longer describe it. Then the teacher has to score the altered image itself, which means keeping the teacher running during training. Saved scores with mild augmentation is the practical middle, and it is what the lab uses.

## A model with distillation in its history

The student, DeiT, has a place in this subject already. Its name stands for data-efficient image transformer. When it was introduced, vision transformers were thought to need enormous datasets, and DeiT showed that one could be trained well on far less with the help of a teacher. The small student you are about to distill into was itself made practical by distillation.

## What to expect, and what not to assume

With four labelled photos per breed, the baseline student has very little to go on, and a strong zero-shot teacher should be well ahead of it. This is the opposite starting position from the banking task, where the baseline had a fair chance of matching the prompted teacher. Whether that holds on your run is for the lab to show. If it does, there is a wide gap for distillation to close, and the question becomes how much of it a model seventy times smaller can cover.

<ReviewCard

>
  Dividing scores by the temperature shrinks the teacher term's gradients by about T squared. Multiplying back keeps the teacher term and the label term on a comparable scale.
